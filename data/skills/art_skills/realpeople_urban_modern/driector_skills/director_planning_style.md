# Urban Style Constraints · Live-Action Urban Realism · Technique Reference

---

## I. Color Grading System and Visual Tone

- **Color Base** — The entire film uses off-white (C1), warm skin tones (C2), and premium gray (C6) as base colors. The overall color temperature is slightly warm (5200-5800K), with medium-low saturation (40-60%), presenting a warm and bright urban lifestyle feel.
- **Warm/Cold Narrative Contrast** — Warm colors (Amber Warm C7, Cream Pink C5, Metallic Silver C8) serve as visual signals for emotional escalation and relationship confirmation. Cold colors are used for segments involving pressure, misunderstanding, or alienation. The shift between warm and cold tones should synchronize with the emotional arc.
- **Warm/Cold Allocation Strategy** — Daily and work segments should primarily use neutral/slightly cool tones, while intimate and reconciliation segments should release warm tones. Avoid high warmth throughout the entire film to prevent the collapse of emotional layers.
- **Palette-First Principle** — Segment planning should first bind to emotional scenes (encounter/ambiguity/date/argument/reconciliation, etc.), then determine the primary + secondary colors and lighting scheme, avoiding situations where "the plot is right but the emotional color is wrong."
- **Prohibited Color Domains** — High-saturation fluorescent colors, strong neon pollution, and excessive cyber colors are incompatible with this style.

## II. Lighting Scheme System

- **Lighting as Narrative** — Six lighting schemes correspond to different emotional segments. During the director's planning phase, the lighting tone direction should be determined at the segment level, rather than specified shot by shot.
- **Light Source Angle** — Front-facing shots default to a 30° oblique angle (softer), while side-facing shots are fine-tuned based on the scene and character relationships.

| Lighting Scheme | Scheme Name | Color Tendency | Applicable Emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Natural Diffusion | Off-white base + uniform soft light | Daily commute, slice-of-life narrative |
| B | Warm-Toned Side Light | Cream warm + localized warm light | Escalating interaction, domestic relaxation |
| C | Top Light Ambient Mix | Neutral gray + soft cool white | Workplace rhythm, rational communication |
| D | Window Light Point Source | Amber warm dominant + cool blue background | Nighttime ambiguity, intimate progression |
| E | Street Neon Glow | Cold background + warm accents | Urban night scenes, romantic wandering |
| F | Overcast Diffused Cold Light | Gray-blue dominant + localized warm tones | Transition segments, low-tide segments |

- **Warm/Cold Light Allocation** — Warm light can be used throughout the film but requires layered control. Reduce the proportion of warm light in conflict/alienation segments to avoid conflict between emotion and light color.
- **Atmosphere Direction Mapping** — The atmosphere direction of each scene should map to one of the above lighting schemes (A-F) to ensure visual consistency.

## III. Texture Direction

- **Ultra-Clear Documentary Feel** — Core of live-action realism photography: visible skin texture, distinct individual hair strands, and clear, credible material details.
- **High Contrast + Extreme Detail** — Serves as the anchor for visual texture. It is not low-contrast "fresh" style, nor heavy-filter atmospheric style, but a filmic documentary feel that is shootable and implementable.
- **Material Authenticity** — Costumes, makeup, and props must possess realistic credibility: suit fabrics, glass reflections, metal components, wooden furniture, and electronic screen highlights must all conform to urban live-action logic.
- **Skin Texture** — Retain real pores and minor imperfections, control the intensity of skin smoothing, aiming for "real but beautiful."
- **Realism Does Not Mean Flatness** — Amplify emotional impact through composition, shot scale, and movement rhythm, rather than relying on exaggerated special effects and excessive post-production.

## IV. Urban Scene Spatial Elements

Scene elements unique to the urban worldview and their visual narrative functions:

- **Window Frames/Glass/Doorways** — Natural framing composition props, creating spatial layering and a sense of distance in character relationships.
- **Streets/Subways/Elevators/Office Building Corridors** — Carriers of urban rhythm; the switch between commuting and pausing can directly map to the character's psychological state.
- **Cafés/Offices/Home Living Rooms** — Emotional containers: Cafés lean toward probing, offices lean toward restraint, homes lean toward safety and relaxation.
- **Weather and Time of Day as Transitions** — Day to night, clear to rainy, dusk to night can be used for emotional buffering between segments, avoiding hard cuts.
- **Visuals Over Explanation for Turning Points** — Prioritize using visual means (sudden shot scale changes, line-of-sight misalignment, spatial barriers) to handle emotional turning points, reducing the burden of dialogue-based explanation.

## V. Urban Instruments and Ambient Sound

Sound element constraints under the urban worldview:

### Instrument Selection

- **Piano** — Core instrument for daily, warm, and memory segments; best reflects the texture of urban life.
- **Guitar** — Effective lead for relaxed, street, and slow-paced romantic segments.
- **Strings** — Main underpinning for emotional escalation and emotional outburst segments.
- **Lo-fi/Light Electronic** — Style enhancer for youthful, urban nightlife, and rhythm-switching segments.
- **High Priority for Ambient Sound** — In urban dramas, real ambient sound is key to immersion; music should not overpower dialogue.

### Instrument Combination Strategy

| Emotional Stage | Instrument Combination |
|---|---|
| Stable/Opening/Closing | Piano solo or Guitar solo |
| Daily Progression | Piano + Light Percussion |
| Ambiguity Escalation/Romance | Piano + Strings |
| Emotional Outburst/Conflict | Strings dominant + Low-frequency underpinning |
| Night Scene/Transition | Lo-fi/Light Electronic + Ambient Sound |

### Urban Ambient Sound

- **Typical Ambient Sound Layers** — Traffic noise / Subway arrival announcements / Elevator chimes / Coffee machine sounds / Keyboard typing / Rain sounds / Air conditioning hum / Distant city noise.
- **Label 1-2 core ambient sounds for each scene** to assist subsequent sound design. The more accurate the ambient sound layers, the stronger the urban realism texture.

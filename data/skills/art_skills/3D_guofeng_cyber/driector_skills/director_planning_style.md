## I. Color Palette System and Visual Tone
(Dual-scene self-check: Both Ancient and Urban scenes have clear execution standards, with no adaptation blind spots)

- **Color Base** — The entire film uses Moon White (C1), Cyan-Green (C2), and Indigo Blue (C4) as the Eastern traditional base colors, overlaid with Chinese-style adapted cyber core colors (Vermilion Neon, Cyan-Green Fluorescence, Indigo Cyber Light, Gilded Holographic Color); overall color temperature is neutral to cool (4500-5800K), traditional base saturation is medium-high (55-75%), and cyber overlay saturation is controllably high (70-90%). [Ancient Scene] Base colors are the core colors for architecture, costumes, and props; [Urban Scene] The same color system serves as the base for skyscraper facades, cityscapes, public spaces, and costumes/props, with cyber overlay colors uniformly anchored to the Eastern traditional color system, eliminating chaotic urban neon without Chinese-style anchors.
- **Emotional Palette Drivers** — Six dual-scene universal emotional palettes correspond to different narrative segments; each palette features a dual-track system of "Traditional Base Layer + Cyber Overlay Layer," and palette switching must be fully synchronized with the story arc:
  1. Court Opulence / Urban Power Center
  2. Landscape Aesthetics / Urban Aerial Gardens
  3. Boudoir Gentleness / Urban Private Residences
  4. Wuxia Severity / Urban Workplace Confrontation / Criminal Investigation Games
  5. Festive Joy / Urban Street Festivals / Nightlife Narrative
  6. Moonlit Serenity / Urban Night Solitude / City Empty Shots
- **Warm/Cool Narrative Contrast** — Traditional warm colors (Vermilion C3, Golden Yellow C5, Rouge C7, Gamboge C9) + Cyber warm light (Neon Vermilion, Holographic Gold) serve as visual signals for narrative turning points, used for emotional heating and festive segments; Traditional cool colors (Indigo Blue C4, Ink Black C6) + Cyber cool light (Fluorescent Cyan-Green, Cyber Indigo) are used for severe, melancholic, and serene segments; The brightness/color switching of cyber light can serve as a visual anchor for strong narrative turns. [Ancient Scene] Traditional colors are primary, cyber colors are secondary; [Urban Scene] The weight of the dual-track colors can be balanced according to narrative needs.
- **Palette-First Principle** — Segment planning must first bind the emotional scene, then determine the "Traditional Main Color + Secondary Color + Cyber Overlay Color" and lighting scheme, avoiding "the plot is right but the emotional color is wrong," ensuring the color logic of Ancient/Urban scenes is completely unified.
- **Forbidden Color Zones** — High-saturation chaotic fluorescent colors without Chinese-style adaptation, incongruous modern digital colors, low-quality cheap neon colors, and Western cyberpunk illogical color clashes are incompatible with this style; [Urban Scene] additionally forbidden: street advertising color jumps without Chinese-style anchors, chaotic street neon, and pure industrial-style cool gray tones.

## II. Lighting Scheme System
(Dual-scene self-check: All 7 schemes are fully adapted for Ancient + Urban, each with clear dual-scene usage, no execution blind spots)

- **Lighting as Narrative** — 7 Chinese-style cyber dual-track lighting schemes correspond to different emotional segments; during the director's planning phase, the tonal direction of "Traditional Base Light + Cyber Overlay Light" should be determined at the segment level, rather than specifying illogically shot by shot. Ancient/Urban scenes can directly reuse the same scheme framework.
- **3D Rendering Lighting Core Features** — Volumetric light, Ambient Occlusion (AO), and depth-of-field blur are the core lighting techniques for the Chinese-style base; newly added holographic light effects, neon contour light, cyber screen reflections, and energy volumetric light are the core lighting techniques for the cyber layer; [Urban Scene] additional adaptations: building neon contour light, multi-layer reflections on glass curtain walls, volumetric light in urban canyon streets, holographic advertising area light sources, backlighting from skyscraper skylines, and controlled light sources in underground spaces. All lighting schemes must reflect PBR physical material rendering texture, ensuring unified lighting logic and physical credibility between the traditional and cyber layers.

|---|---|---|---|
| A | Cyber Warm Radiance | Vermilion traditional base + Golden holographic highlights + Moon White base + Vermilion neon contour light | [Ancient Scene] Court opulence, solemn grandeur, festive magnificence, power narrative<br>[Urban Scene] Top-tier power spaces, skyscraper corporate headquarters, city landmark celebrations, core narrative climax |
| B | Holographic Cyan-Green Aesthetics | Cyan-Green traditional base + Moon White mist volumetric light + Cyan-Green fluorescent diffusion + Holographic landscape light and shadow | [Ancient Scene] Landscape aesthetics, poetic distance, ethereal drift, cyber Zen<br>[Urban Scene] Aerial Chinese-style gardens, urban ecological landmarks, holographic landscape views, Zen office spaces |

- **Warm/Cool Light Allocation** — Traditional warm light (Vermilion/Golden Yellow/Gamboge) + Cyber warm light (Neon Vermilion/Holographic Gold) applies to opulent, gentle, and festive segments; Traditional cool light (Indigo/Ink Black) + Cyber cool light (Fluorescent Cyan-Green/Cyber Indigo) applies to severe, melancholic, and serene segments. Directors can adjust warm/cool switching points based on narrative needs; the on/off of cyber light can serve as a visual signal for strong narrative nodes. Ancient/Urban scenes follow the same warm/cool logic.
- **Atmosphere Direction Mapping** — The atmosphere direction of each scene should map to one of the above lighting schemes (A-G), ensuring complete consistency between the traditional and cyber visual logic, with unified style throughout Ancient/Urban scenes.

## III. Texture Direction
(Dual-scene self-check: Full coverage of Ancient/Urban materials, clear dual-scene aging texture standards, eliminating plastic/CG feel)

- **3D Rendering as Dual-Track Anchor** — The core of Chinese-style Cyber 3D: high-precision dual-track modeling, PBR physical material rendering, traditional layer volumetric light + cyber layer holographic light, ambient occlusion, depth-of-field blur, presenting cinematic 3D rendering throughout. [Ancient Scene] Focus on high-precision modeling of ancient architecture, traditional costumes/props, and natural landscapes; [Urban Scene] Focus on high-precision dual-track modeling of New Chinese-style skyscrapers, urban public spaces, Chinese-style cyber vehicles, and urban costumes/props. Both scene sets strictly follow the same PBR rendering standard.
- **PBR Material Dual-Track Fusion** — All costumes, props, and scene assets must achieve credible texture through PBR physical rendering, with the core being "organic fusion of traditional + cyber materials," covering both scenes:
  - [Ancient Core Materials] Silk sheen and drape, wood grain and patina, metal reflection and texture, jade transparency and warmth, porcelain glaze sheen
  - [Urban Core Materials] Transparent reflection of glass curtain walls and light/shadow cuts of Chinese-style grilles, fusion of reinforced concrete texture and traditional stone texture, matte texture of metal aluminum panels and aged reflection of traditional copper ornaments, wear marks on asphalt roads echoing weathered textures of bluestone, flowing light of fiber optic fabrics unified with the drape of traditional silk
  - All fused materials must be physically logical and self-consistent; meaningless cyber material stacking is prohibited.
- **Volumetric Light and Depth-of-Field Dual-Track Reinforcement** — Volumetric light is the soul of Chinese-style cyber visuals: [Ancient Scene] Outdoor scenes must have dual volumetric light scattering from air perspective and traditional skylight + cyber neon; indoor scenes create multi-layer volumetric light effects through window light/candlelight/holographic projections; [Urban Scene] Outdoor scenes must have volumetric light in urban canyon streets, skylight diffusion from skyscraper clusters, and volumetric scattering of neon lights; indoor scenes create multi-layer volumetric light effects through window lattice projections/holographic screens/controlled light sources. Depth-of-field blur synchronously reinforces spatial depth in both scenes, adapting to different spatial scales of Ancient/Urban settings.
- **Aging Texture Dual-Track Unity** — Materials cannot be too clean or perfect; both scenes must reflect traces of life and aging:
  - [Ancient Scene] Wood surfaces have usage marks, stone surfaces have weathering patterns and moss, fabrics have natural wrinkles, roof tiles have moss stains and missing pieces
  - [Urban Scene] Asphalt roads have tire wear, glass curtain walls have water stains and weathering marks, metal facades have oxidation and scratches, street facilities have usage marks, neon signs have local flickering defects
  - "Plastic feel," brand-new flawless "cheap CG feel," and "showroom-style" urban visuals without life traces are prohibited throughout.
- **Cyber is Not Cold** — Chinese-style Cyber 3D emphasizes the balance between the warmth of Eastern aesthetics and the avant-garde feel of cyber sci-fi, conveying emotion through dual-track material textures, layered lighting, and traditional + cyber fusion color palettes, rather than relying on meaningless spectacle effects and violent cyber element stacking. Both Ancient/Urban scenes must uphold the core of Eastern aesthetics.

## IV. Chinese-Style Cyber Scene Spatial Elements
(Dual-scene self-check: All elements adapted for dual scenes, new urban-exclusive Chinese-style cyber elements added, full coverage of Ancient/Urban narrative functions)

Unique dual-track scene elements of the Chinese-style cyber worldview and their visual narrative functions, with the core being "organic fusion of traditional Eastern spatial logic + cyber sci-fi elements," **100% adapted for Ancient traditional scenes and Chinese-style cyber urban scenes**, prohibiting illogical element stacking:

- **Framed Composition Elements** — [Ancient Scene] Sheer curtains/Chinese-style screens/Chinese-style door frames; [Urban Scene] Holographic sheer curtain partitions/controlled Chinese-style screens/AR Chinese-style door frames/semi-transparent controlled glass curtain walls. Natural framed composition props, creating a sense of "interplay of virtual and real" and spatial depth. In 3D rendering, light penetration effects of semi-transparent materials, virtual/real switching of holographic projections, and narrative boundary cuts of frames are core visual highlights, usable for virtual/real transitions, memory flashbacks, and identity metaphors in the narrative.
- **Negative Space Aesthetic Elements** — [Ancient Scene] Chinese-style courtrards/traditional flower trees/natural rain curtains/wind through bamboo groves; [Urban Scene] Aerial Chinese-style gardens/rooftop holographic flower trees/vertical green landscapes/controlled rain curtains/urban street rain scenes/cloud seas between skyscrapers. Natural carriers for negative space composition; the scene is the emotion: full courtyard of blooming flowers = relief, sitting alone in the rain = solitude, falling leaves = parting sorrow. In 3D scenes, the volumetric feel and light/shadow interaction of vegetation, light refraction of rain curtains, and overlay effects of virtual/real scenes are particularly important.
- **Light Source Carrier Elements** — [Ancient Scene] Candle flames/natural moonlight/wooden lattice window light/lanterns; [Urban Scene] Holographic Chinese-style candle flames/artificial cyber moonlight/controlled Chinese-style lattice projection light/skyscraper lattice area light sources/Chinese-style neon lanterns. Dual-track light source carriers in the Chinese-style cyber world, with traditional and cyber light sources corresponding one-to-one: candlelight/holographic candlelight = warm/private (Scheme C), moonlight/artificial cyber moonlight = cool/serene (Scheme F), window light/controlled lattice light = daily/quiet (Scheme E). In 3D rendering, the dual volumetric light effect of light sources and the traditional + cyber dual reflection of PBR materials are the core of visual texture.
- **Architectural Signature Elements** — [Ancient Scene] Traditional flying eaves/wooden dougong (bracket sets)/blue tile roofs/mortise-and-tenon structures; [Urban Scene] New Chinese-style skyscraper flying eaves/steel structure mechanical dougong/Chinese-style element facades/blue tile texture metal roofs/mortise-and-tenon steel structures. Signature elements of Chinese-style cyber architecture; 3D modeling must simultaneously reflect the exquisite details of traditional carved beams and painted pillars and the industrial beauty of cyber mechanical structures. Materials must simultaneously present aging traces of the traditional layer and wear/oxidation of the cyber layer; illogical mechanical modifications detached from ancient architectural forms are prohibited.
- **Street Narrative Core Elements** — [Ancient Scene] Ancient streets/alleys/street shops/archways; [Urban Scene] Chinese-style cyber streets/skyscraper canyons/New Chinese-style street shops/Chinese-style neon sign streets/holographic Chinese-style archways. Core spatial carriers for street narratives in both scenes; 3D rendering must reflect spatial depth of streets, unity of Chinese-style elements, and the life feel of street hustle and bustle. These are core scenes for ensemble narratives and daily segments.
- **Segment Transition Rules** — This style has rich dual-scene scene assets (variants of different times/weather/seasons/cyberization levels); segment transitions are recommended to use scene empty shots for emotional buffering, avoiding hard cuts. [Ancient Scene] Adapted for courtyard/landscape/architecture empty shots; [Urban Scene] Adapted for skyscraper skyline/aerial gardens/city landmarks/street empty shots/subway/aerial corridor empty shots.
- **Narrative Priority Rules** — Use visuals rather than dialogue for turning points; prioritize visual means (lighting mutations, shot size jump cuts, virtual/real empty shot metaphors, holographic projection content switching, cyber element on/off) rather than relying on dialogue explanations. Ancient/Urban scenes follow the same narrative logic.

## V. Chinese-Style Cyber Instruments and Environmental Sound
(Dual-scene self-check: Full coverage of dual-scene sound systems, clear adaptation schemes for Ancient/Urban emotions, no incongruity)

Dual-track sound element constraints under the Chinese-style cyber worldview, with the core being "organic fusion of traditional Eastern instrument core + cyber electronic music," **synchronously adapted for Ancient scenes and Chinese-style cyber urban scenes**, prohibiting incongruous electronic music stacking:

### Instrument Selection
(Universal for dual scenes, directly reusable for Ancient/Urban narratives)
- **Xiao (Flute)** — Core instrument for desolate, solitary, and sorrowful segments; can be paired with electronic delay effects to enhance the cool, mournful cyber atmosphere; best expresses cold, mournful feelings
- **Erhu** — Segments of emotional agitation, grief, and longing; the crying tone of the strings suits emotional outbursts; can be paired with distortion effects to enhance the cyber narrative tension of fate
- **Suona** — Segments of intense emotional fluctuation (great sorrow/joy, fate turning points, climax); use with caution, but once used it is a nuclear bomb; can be paired with synthesizer low-frequency noise to enhance the extreme emotional impact of the cyber world
- **Guqin** — Opening tone setting / steady segments; used with Xiao to reflect landscape aesthetics; can be paired with ambient electronic pads to enhance the ethereal feel of cyber Zen
- **Pipa** — Accent for tense, urgent segments; applicable to Wuxia severity/urban game scenes; can be paired with industrial electronic drum beats to enhance sharp rhythmic feel
- **Guzheng** — Atmospheric instrument for Court Opulence/Urban Festival segments; gorgeous and elegant; can be paired with electronic synthesizer arpeggios to enhance grand atmosphere
- String music underlay can increase cinematic feel but should not overshadow the main theme; electronic synthesizer pads must be anchored to the tonality of traditional instruments; illogical electronic sound effects detached from the Chinese-style core are prohibited

### Chinese-Style Cyber Environmental Sound
(Dual-scene layered design, clear execution standards for Ancient/Urban scenes)

- **Dual-Scene Universal Environmental Sound Base Layer** — Cicada chirps/insect songs / babbling streams / wind through bamboo groves / Chinese-style street vendor calls / night rain dripping on eaves / fabric friction / light chime of Chinese-style wind bells / bird chirps / falling leaves rustling
- **Chinese-Style Cyber Urban Exclusive Environmental Sound Layers** — [Traditional Layer] Street folk music background, wind through Chinese-style grilles, chime of Chinese-style signs, street vendor calls; [Cyber Layer] Skyscraper wind noise, low hum of traffic, low-frequency sound of subway operation, hum of neon signs, light human voice of holographic ads, light sound of controlled equipment, wind noise of glass curtain walls
- **Execution Standards** — [Ancient Scene] Mark 1-2 traditional core environmental sounds for each scene; [Urban Scene] Mark 1 traditional core environmental sound + 1 urban cyber core environmental sound for each scene, to assist subsequent sound design. The more the dual-track environmental sound layers match the narrative, the stronger the immersion of the Chinese-style cyber scene.

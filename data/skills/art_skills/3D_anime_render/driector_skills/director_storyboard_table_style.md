# Storyboard Table 3D Animation Rendering Constraints · 3D Cartoon Rendering · Technique Reference

---

## I. Storyboard Table Positioning

The storyboard table is the core tool for the director to translate the script into camera language. The following provides only the constraint specifications for the 3D cartoon rendering style at the storyboard table level.

---

## II. Lighting and Atmosphere

- **Unified Lighting Within a Scene** — No more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is a clear narrative turning point (e.g., turning off a desk lamp → cold moonlight glow)
- **Emotional Color Palette Binding** — Each scene must be bound to at least one emotional context (e.g., romantic encounter / daily warmth / parting sorrow), and maintain continuity of primary and secondary colors within the shot group
- **Light Source Angle Standards** — Default to 45° side-backlight to simulate the effect of twilight. When describing lighting, the difference between front-facing and side-facing angles must be distinguished
- **Warm/Cool Tones Match Narrative Stages** — The first half (daily life / work / study) should maintain warm tones; cool tones (cool blue + warm color contrast) should be used at night or after an emotional turning point
- **Lighting Transitions are an Advanced Technique** — Gradual shift from natural light (A) to indoor warm light (B) = passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## III. Environmental Dynamics

- **Environmental Dynamics Add a Sense of Breath to the Frame** — Swaying leaves, flowing clouds, passing traffic, falling raindrops, flickering lights. Arrange at least one shot with environmental dynamics every 3-4 shots to avoid the frame feeling "dead"
- **Urban Environmental Elements Take Priority** — Environmental dynamics should use elements within the urban worldview: swaying leaves, flowing clouds, passing traffic, flickering neon, fluttering curtains. Elements incompatible with the 3D animation style are prohibited

---

## IV. 3D Animation Action Rhythm

- **Actions Must Be Natural** — All character actions default to a natural rhythm. Rising, turning, and raising hands should be marked as "natural" or "gentle"
- **Use Implication for Sweet Interactions** — Eye contact, slight finger touches, fingertip contact when handing over items. Do not write large-scale two-person interactions in the storyboard table; break them down into suggestive close-up shots
- **3D Animation Fluidity** — The unique "inter-frame fluidity" style of 3D animation; actions should be continuous and natural, avoiding stiff frame jumps

---

## V. Camera Movement Prohibitions

- **Prohibit Fast Camera Movements** — Whips, rapid pushes, and fast cuts conflict with the temperament of 3D cartoon rendering (unless used for transitions or emphasis)
- **Prohibit Fancy Transitions** — Wipes, rotations, venetian blinds, etc., are incompatible with this style

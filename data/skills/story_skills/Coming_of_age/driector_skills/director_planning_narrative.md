# Narrative Technique · Youth Growth · Technical Reference

---

## 1. Theme conception and emotional core

### Key points of youth growth narrative

- **The Preciousness of the First Time** — The core charm of youth narrative lies in the impact of the "first time": the first heartbeat, the first failure, the first time to say goodbye, the first time to stand up for others. The theme should be centered around the intensity and panic of "experience some emotion for the first time in your life"
- **Awkwardness is real** — The expression of youth is inherently clumsy. Wrong words said, wrong things done, the courage to miss and try hard to get back - perfect youth is not moving, only those who are in a hurry are moving.
- **Irreversible time is the underlying emotion** — All the sweetness and regret of youth narratives have a common background: "You can never go back." This sense of irreversibility gives weight to every tiny moment
- **Suggested directions for leaving the scene** — nostalgia/relief/smiling with tears/wanting to go back to that day. Avoid utilitarian directions such as "feeling good" and "counterattack"
- **Growing Pains** — Youth narratives can’t just be sweet. Real growth comes from pain: cracks in friendships, shattered ideals, realizing for the first time that "the world is not what I thought it was." Sweetness is the surface, pain is the engine of growth

## 2. Narrative structure and rhythm planning

### Key points of youth growth narrative

- **Fragmented narrative is a natural form** — Memories of youth are inherently fragmented: a sentence between classes, the sunset on the way to school, the sudden rain one day. The narrative structure can accept a more fragmented paragraph organization, connected by emotional lines rather than event lines.
- **Emotional roller coaster** — The emotional changes of youth are drastic: one second you laugh until your stomach hurts, the next second your eyes are red. The emotional switching between paragraphs can be more rapid than other types, which itself is the quality of youth.
- **The growth arc is not a straight line** — The character's growth is not a straight line of "I don't understand → I understand", but a twist and turn of "I thought I understood → I was slapped in the face → I really understand". Every time I think I have grown up, I realize that I am still far behind
- **Dual lines of memories and the present** — Youth narratives often use the double line structure of "looking back at the past now". The memory line is full of vivid details, and the current line is responsible for giving these details the weight of "never going back"
- **The intersection and separation of group portraits** — The core narrative model of youth group portraits is "getting together → experiencing together → going their separate ways". The most tear-jerking thing is not the separation itself, but "we thought we would be together forever"
- **"That Day" Model** — Each important turning point in the youth narrative should be bound to a specific "that day": it rained heavily that day, it was the sports day, and school ended very early that day. Specific time anchor points make memories tactile

## 3. Scene emotional design

### Key points of youth growth narrative

- **Use youthful somatosensory words for emotional goals** — Don’t say "happy", say "laugh until you bend over and then suddenly look at each other and freeze". Don't say "sad", say "don't dare to shed tears until you leave school". The emotions of youth are always mixed and contradictory
- **Typical emotional passages and designs** —

| Paragraph Types | Emotional Direction | Narrative Techniques | Music Suggestions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily fun | Vitality + worry-free | Group play, fragmented daily life, fast-paced editing | Brisk rhythm, guitar/whistle/hand clapping |
| The bud of secret love | Heartbeat + tension | Peeping, deliberately walking the same road, touching the electric shock of your fingers | Soft melody, the rhythm hints at the heartbeat |
| Friendship test | Grievance + stubbornness | Misunderstanding, cold war, eating lunch alone, pretending to be fine | Reduce the soundtrack, and the ambient sound highlights isolation |
| Fighting/Competition | Tension + Passion | Countdown pressure, teamwork, last fight | The drum beats get stronger and the rhythm accelerates |
| Farewell/Graduation | Reluctance + Relief | Empty the drawer, walk together for the last time, autograph book | From lively to quiet, and finally only one voice remains |
| Looking back/Growth | Nostalgia + Warmth | Returning to the old place after many years, opening old things, it suddenly feels like yesterday | Warm instrumental music slowly enters, with a hint of desolation |

- **"Spatial memory binding" is the core tool of narrative** — In youth narratives, specific spaces should be bound to specific emotions, and repeated occurrences form emotional resonance:
  - **Classroom/Playground**: Daily/Collective Memory/Sense of Belonging
  - **The Road Home**: A space for secret love/solitude/worrying
  - **Secret Base** (rooftop/under the tree/old warehouse): friendship/commitment/a space that belongs only to us
- **Season is Emotion** — Make good use of seasonal changes to convey the passage of time and emotional transition: Spring = budding/first meeting; Summer = enthusiasm/conflict; Autumn = separation/maturity; Winter = precipitation/looking back
- **"The person next to you" perspective** - The advanced technique of youth narrative is "seeing the protagonist's changes from the people next to you" - the back of the teacher seen outside the window, the changes in expressions secretly noticed by friends, parents discovering that their children no longer need to be picked up and dropped off

## 4. Sound and Music Direction

### Key points of youth growth narrative

- **Youth has its own soundscape** — the school bell, the friction of sneakers on the floor, the sound of turning books, the distant whistle of the playground, the click of bicycle chains. These sounds constitute the "sound texture of youth", and each scene should be marked with 1-2 iconic youth sound effects
- **Music is the anchor point of memory** — The theme music in youth narratives has a special function: when the same melody sounds in different paragraphs, the audience will automatically recall the previous emotional memory. It is recommended to use different arrangements of the same topic in key paragraphs
- **Silent Growth** — The moment when a character truly "grows up" is often silent: no longer arguing, no longer chasing, just standing there and watching. The soundtrack should be removed from these growth nodes and only the ambient sound should be left
- **Environmental sound is the era** - Use ambient sound to convey the background of the era: radio gymnastics music in the classroom, pop songs from street shops, and the chirping of cicadas in summer. Sound can evoke the resonance of the times more than images
- **Music Emotion Progressive Model** —

| Emotional Stages | Music Strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Daily hilarity | Brisk rhythm, energetic instrumental music | Medium to high |
| Undercurrent of thoughts | Soft melody, low volume | Mid-low |
| Conflict breaks out | Music suddenly stops or rapid tempo | Extreme |
| Reconciliation and Recovery | Warm variations of the theme melody | Medium |
| Farewell and departure | Gradient from group ensemble to single instrument | Medium → Low |

- **The distance between laughter and crying** — The most touching sound effect processing in the youth narrative is "laughter suddenly turns into sobs" or "crying and laughing out". Quick switching of emotions does not require soundtrack assistance, natural vocals are enough

## 5. Composition and scenery narrative

### Key points of youth growth narrative

- **Narrative function of three core compositions** —
  - **Group portrait composition** — multiple people in the same frame/side by side/sitting around, conveying the sense of belonging and collective memory of "we are together"
  - **Front and back composition** — One person is in front and the other is behind, conveying the secret love/gaze of "looking at you but you don't know"
  - **Symmetrical composition** — two people walking side by side/sitting on the same bench, conveying "equal companionship"
- **Scenery distinction changes with relationship** — When the characters first meet, the scenery distinction is far away (each is in a different position in the picture); after the relationship heats up, the scenery distinction tightens (same frame/close shot); when they separate, the scenery distinction becomes distant again (these distant views are cut in half)
- **Emotional Memory of Space** — When the same space appears repeatedly in different paragraphs, the composition should change subtly to convey the emotional changes: the same road from school, from two people walking side by side to one person walking alone; the same classroom seat, from full to empty
- **The dual function of long shots** — In youth narratives, long shots can convey both "freedom/broadness" (running on the playground, overlooking the rooftop) and "insignificance/loneliness" (a person walking on an empty street after school).
- **Youth function of close-up** — Close-up is used to capture the fleeting micro-expressions of youth: the corners of the snickering mouth, the tips of red ears, the eyelashes blinking away tears.
- **Perspective anchoring of "that day"** - The composition of the recall paragraph can deliberately adopt the "perspective in memory": imperfect angles, partial occlusion, as if it was secretly seen from a certain position

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points of youth growth narrative

- **The alternation of movement and stillness is the rhythm of youth** — The lens rhythm of youth narrative should switch frequently between "vibrant dynamics" and "quiet gaze". Running → suddenly stopping → looking back, this rhythm itself is youth
- **Running with the camera = the iconic shot of youth** — Running with the character (chasing the bus/chasing people/racing/sprinting) is the most recognizable shot of youth narratives. Stay slightly unstable when following the shot, conveying the vitality of "desperately moving forward"
- **Slow push = Discover/Notice** — The psychological suggestion of "noting that person", suitable for having a crush, finding a friend crying, or noticing a certain change
- **Slow down = leave/grow up** — the psychological suggestion of "leave this place/this person", suitable for saying goodbye, graduation, and realizing that everything is different
- **Still Gaze = Moment of Growth** — The moment when the character really grows up, the camera should stop and watch quietly. Not pushing, not pulling, not shaking, just watching this person change at this moment
- **Mirror movement is emotional temperature** — Shake and follow = vitality/chaos; Smooth and slow movement = Warmth/daily; Stillness = Contemplation/Growth; Slowly zoom out = Farewell/Relief
